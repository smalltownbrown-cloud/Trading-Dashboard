VWAP Pullback
Strategy 1 · Ben's #1 Day Trade Setup · Golden Hour
The Big Idea
A strong stock pulls back to VWAP and bounces. You're buying the dip at the most important intraday price level — where the most volume traded. If buyers are still in control, this level holds.
VWAP Pullback · 5-Min Chart
What to Look For
  • Stock ran up strong in the morning — making higher highs
  • Price pulls back toward VWAP (purple/pink line on chart)
  • A green candle forms AT or just above VWAP — this is the bounce candle
  • That bounce candle closes back above VWAP — buyers defending the level
  • Volume picks up on the bounce candle vs the red pullback candles
✅ Valid Setup
Green bounce candle at VWAP, closes above it, volume ticks up. Stock held VWAP all morning = buyers are strong. This is your entry. Strike: nearest call above current price. Expiry: this week or next.
❌ Failed Setup
Price touches VWAP but the candle CLOSES below it. That's a breakdown, not a bounce. Do NOT enter — VWAP failed as support. Wait for a fresh setup or move on.
⏰ When to Look
Golden Hour (1–3 PM CT). You've watched the stock all day. Morning runners that hold VWAP all session and then pull back in the afternoon are the cleanest setups.
Demand Zone Bounce
Strategy 4 · Smart Money Follow · Support Confirmation
The Big Idea
A demand zone is a price level where big buyers showed up before. When price returns to that level, those same buyers often defend it again. The scanner's sweep + a demand zone = double confirmation.
Demand Zone Bounce · Daily Chart
What to Look For
  • Find the previous high that became support — a range where price bounced before
  • Draw a box around that level — that's the demand zone
  • Wait for price to pull back INTO that zone
  • Look for a green rejection candle forming inside the zone
  • Scanner sweep fires at or just above the zone — BIG confirmation
✅ Valid Setup
Price enters demand zone, forms a hammer or green engulfing candle, and closes back above the zone's top line. Scanner confirms with sweep. This is your AMZN $255–257 setup in action.
❌ Failed Setup
Price enters the zone but keeps falling through it on heavy volume. Zone is broken. Don't buy into a falling knife. Wait for a new level or a different setup.
Resistance Breakout
Strategy 4 · Ben's Trigger Line · Pre-Plan the Entry
The Big Idea
Ben draws a horizontal line at a key resistance level — the price where selling has stopped the stock before. When price CLOSES above that line with volume, resistance became support. That's the trigger.
Breakout · 5-Min Chart
What to Look For
  • Draw a horizontal line at the previous high — that's resistance
  • Watch for price to "coil" just below that line (tighter and tighter)
  • The breakout candle CLOSES above the resistance line — not just wicks above it
  • Volume on the breakout candle is higher than the previous candles
  • Ben's WATCH alert price is usually right at this resistance line
✅ Valid Breakout
Candle BODY closes above resistance. Volume is 2x+ normal. This is Ben's trigger line moment — when price crosses it with a green close, that's when BOUGHT gets sent.
❌ False Breakout
Candle wicks above resistance but BODY closes below. This is a "fakeout." Sellers still in control. Do not enter on wicks. Wait for a full candle body close above the line.
SPY Rubber Band
Strategy 2 · Daily Chart · Keltner Channel
The Big Idea
SPY always returns to its average. When it stretches 3x its normal daily range (3 ATR), it's exhausted and snaps back. Ben has documented this happening 100% of the time in the past year.
SPY Rubber Band · Daily Chart · Keltner Channel
The ATR Bands Explained
1 ATR = Normal 2 ATR = Extended 3 ATR = Extreme ← Your entry
  • Use a DAILY chart — this is not a 5-min pattern
  • Add the Keltner Channel indicator (set multiplier to 1, 2, 3)
  • Wait for SPY to touch the 3 ATR band (red line)
  • Below 3 ATR = buy calls (snapback up to 21 EMA)
  • Above 3 ATR = buy puts (snapback down to 21 EMA)
  • Target = the blue 21 EMA line (the mean)
  • Expiry = 2 weeks out minimum — this takes a few days
📊 Today's Context
SPY is at $754.38, down -0.68%, below VWAP. Check if it's approaching a lower Keltner band on the daily. If it's near 2 ATR below, watch for 3 ATR — that could set up a rubber band calls trade.
🔧 Setting Up Keltner Channels in IBKR
The Problem
IBKR adds all 3 Keltner lines in the same color by default — making it impossible to tell 1 ATR from 2 ATR from 3 ATR. Here's how to fix it.
STEP 1 — Add the indicator 3 times
In IBKR chart → tap the indicators icon → search "Keltner" → add it → then add it again → and again. You need 3 separate instances of the same indicator.
STEP 2 — Set the multipliers
Tap each Keltner instance → Settings/Edit → find the Multiplier field:
■ First instance → Multiplier = 1
■ Second instance → Multiplier = 2
■ Third instance → Multiplier = 3
STEP 3 — Change the colors
Tap each instance → find Color setting → change to:
■ Green = 1 ATR (normal range)
■ Yellow = 2 ATR (extended — watch for exhaustion)
■ Red = 3 ATR (extreme — your entry zone)
Also add a ■ Blue 21 EMA separately — that's the mean/target.
STEP 4 — Use DAILY chart
Switch to the 1D timeframe. The Rubber Band is a daily chart setup — not 5-min. The 3 ATR touch looks very different (and more powerful) on the daily.
✅ What it looks like when set up correctly
Three distinct colored bands around price. Green hugging close, yellow further out, red at the extremes. The blue 21 EMA runs through the middle. When price touches red — that's your signal.
Reading Trend Structure
Foundation Skill · Applies to All Strategies
The Big Idea
Uptrends make higher highs AND higher lows. Downtrends make lower highs AND lower lows. When the pattern breaks — that's the warning sign. Know which direction you're fighting before placing any trade.
Trend Structure · HH/HL vs LH/LL
Uptrend = Calls
Higher High + Higher Low = uptrend intact. Fine to buy calls on pullbacks.
Downtrend = Wait
Lower High + Lower Low = downtrend. Don't fight it with calls.
The Warning Sign — Trend Break
  • You're in an uptrend watching for a VWAP pullback entry
  • Price pulls back but makes a LOWER LOW — not a higher low
  • That's the uptrend structure breaking — don't enter
  • Wait for the stock to prove it can make a new higher high first
34 EMA — Ben's Support Line
Ben uses the 34 EMA on the 5-min chart as a dynamic support level. In strong uptrends, price bounces off the 34 EMA. If price is above the 34 EMA and VWAP — bulls are firmly in control. That's your cleanest entry condition.
Key Candle Patterns
Entry Timing · What Each Candle Type Means
The Big Idea
You don't need to know 50 candle patterns. For Ben's system, you need 4. These four tell you whether buyers or sellers are in control at a specific price level — especially at VWAP and support zones.
The 4 Candles You Need to Know
When to Use Each at VWAP
  • Bullish Engulfing — strongest entry signal. Price dipped, buyers overwhelmed sellers. Enter on close of the green candle.
  • Hammer — buyers rejected the move below VWAP. The long lower wick shows sellers tried and failed. Good entry.
  • Rejection / Shooting Star — at resistance this is a warning. Sellers are still in control. Wait — don't chase.
  • Doji — neither side won. Wait for the NEXT candle to confirm direction before entering.
Volume Confirmation
The Truth Detector · Never Trade Against Volume
The Big Idea
Price moves are only meaningful when volume backs them up. A breakout on low volume is a fakeout. A bounce on high volume is real buyers showing up. Volume is the market's lie detector.
Volume — What to Look For
The Three Volume Rules
  • Breakout candle volume should be 2x+ average — if it breaks resistance on puny volume, it's a trap
  • Pullback volume should be LOWER than breakout volume — light pullbacks = sellers aren't aggressive = healthy
  • Bounce candle volume should spike — at VWAP or demand zone, the bounce needs buyers showing up. No volume = no buyers
Volume + Scanner = Strongest Signal
Your scanner already shows Volume vs Open Interest. When Vol > OI, that's new money. When that lines up with a high-volume candle on the chart — scanner AND chart are both screaming the same thing. That's your highest conviction setup.
Stage 2 Continuation
Strategy 5 · Market Math · Compression → Breakout
The Big Idea
Stage 2 is when a stock transitions from a sideways base into a strong uptrend. Price compresses tightly — coiling like a spring — then breaks out with force. The Point of Control (POC) is the price level where the most volume has traded, acting like a magnet and then a launchpad.
Stage 2 Continuation · Compression → Breakout
The 4 Stages of a Stock
STAGE 1
Accumulation
Flat base. Smart money quietly buying. Boring on the outside.
STAGE 2 ← YOU WANT THIS
Uptrend
Breakout from base. Higher highs, higher lows. EMAs fanning up.
STAGE 3
Distribution
Top forming. Choppy. Smart money selling to retail.
STAGE 4
Downtrend
Lower highs, lower lows. Don't buy calls here.
What to Look For — Stage 2 Checklist
  • Flat base of 4+ weeks — stock has been coiling, building energy
  • Price compresses into tighter and tighter range — the spring loading up
  • POC line below price — the red dotted line in your chart is the highest volume level and acts as a floor
  • EMAs starting to fan upward — 8, 21, 34 EMA all pointing up and spreading apart
  • Breakout candle closes above the range with volume — this is the Stage 2 launch
  • Scanner sweep fires at or just after breakout — smart money confirming
✅ Perfect Entry Timing
The breakout candle closes above the compression range on 2x+ volume. Scanner shows sweep At/Above Ask on that same day. EMAs are fanning up below price. POC is now support below. That's the Stage 2 launch — buy the call on the breakout or the first pullback to the top of the range.
⏰ Timeframe
Stage 2 setups are swing trades — use options expiring 3–6 weeks out minimum. The base took weeks to form, the move takes days to weeks to play out. Short dated contracts will expire before the move completes.
📊 Point of Control in IBKR
Add the Volume Profile indicator to your IBKR chart. The POC shows as a horizontal line at the highest volume node. In Ben's workbook it's shown as the red dotted line. This becomes your stop level — if price breaks back below POC on volume, the setup failed.
Volume Profile & Point of Control
Strategy 5 · Market Math · Ben's POC Red Dotted Line
The Big Idea
The Volume Profile shows WHERE the most trading happened at each price level — displayed as a horizontal histogram on your chart. The longest bar is the Point of Control (POC) — the single price where the most volume traded. Price acts like a magnet toward POC and bounces off it like a floor or ceiling.
Volume Profile · Right Side Display · POC as Support
🔧 Setting Up Volume Profile in IBKR — Right Side Display
STEP 1 — Add the indicator
In IBKR chart → click Studies/Indicators icon → search "Volume Profile" or "Volume by Price" → click Add.
STEP 2 — Move it to the right side
In the indicator settings → find "Display Side" or "Position" → change from Left to Right. This matches Ben's chart setup where the histogram appears on the right edge.
STEP 3 — Identify the POC
The longest horizontal bar in the profile = POC. In IBKR you can enable "Show POC Line" in settings — it draws a horizontal line across the chart at that price level. Change the color to red to match Ben's red dotted line.
STEP 4 — Enable Value Area
In settings enable "Show Value Area" — this highlights the price range where 70% of volume traded (between VAH and VAL). The shaded area = where most action happened. POC sits in the middle of it.
STEP 5 — Add to your chart template
Once set up correctly → Settings gear → Save as Template → overwrite your existing "Merle Omen Setup" template. Now every chart you load will show Volume Profile on the right automatically.
The 3 Key Levels
POC — Point of Control
The longest bar. Where the MOST volume traded. Strongest magnet level. Price always wants to return here. Ben's red dotted line.
VAH — Value Area High
Top of the 70% volume zone. Acts as resistance when price is below it, support when above.
VAL — Value Area Low
Bottom of the 70% zone. Strong support when price returns to it. Often a bounce level.
How Ben Uses POC in Trades
  • POC as entry — price pulls back to POC from above, bounces → buy calls at the touch
  • POC as stop — if price closes BELOW POC on volume, the level failed → exit
  • POC as target — price compressing below POC → breakout above it → T1 = next resistance above
  • Stage 2 confirmation — POC should be BELOW price in a Stage 2 uptrend, acting as a floor under the move
⚡ POC Simple Rule — Memorize This
POC ABOVE price → Resistance
Wait for price to BREAK above it with volume before entering calls. Don't buy into resistance.
POC BELOW price → Floor/Support
Valid entry zone. POC is a floor beneath you. Bounce expected if price pulls back to it.
POC AT price → Congestion
Wait for candle confirmation. Could go either way — let the next candle tell you who won.
✅ Strongest Setup
Scanner sweep fires → stock above VWAP → POC is below current price acting as support → price pulling back to POC → bounce candle forms. That's scanner + VWAP + Volume Profile all confirming the same entry. Highest conviction setup in Ben's system.
⏰ Timeframe Matters
Use a daily chart for the Volume Profile on swing trades — POC over days/weeks is more powerful than intraday. For VWAP pullback day trades use the 5-min chart with session volume profile. Ben typically shows daily profile for his swing setups.
