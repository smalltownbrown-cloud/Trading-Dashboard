What Is an Options Contract?
The foundation of everything Ben trades

An options contract gives you the right to buy a stock at a specific price, by a specific date — without actually owning the stock. You're betting on the direction the stock will move.

The reason traders use options instead of stocks: small stock moves = big option moves. When NVDA moved a few percent, Ben's options moved 40%+ in the same day.

Why this matters
You can control 100 shares of NVDA (worth ~$22,000) for just $245. That's the leverage. But it works both ways — options can also lose value fast, which is why stops matter.
Calls vs. Puts
The two directions you can bet
CALL option
A bet the stock goes UP. You profit when the stock rises above the strike price.
Ben bought NVDA $220 calls → he expected NVDA to rise above $220
PUT option
A bet the stock goes DOWN. You profit when the stock falls below the strike price.
Ben would buy puts if he expected a stock to drop
Ben's style
Ben primarily trades calls — he looks for stocks with strong momentum and unusual order flow pointing upward.
The Three Things That Make Options Move
You don't need to memorize Greek letters — just understand these ideas
  • Stock price moving toward the strike — the biggest driver. If NVDA is at $215 and you own $220 calls, every dollar NVDA moves up makes your option more valuable.
  • Time decay (Theta) — options lose value every single day just from time passing. This is why Ben often uses short expirations — he expects quick moves, not long holds.
  • Volatility (Vega) — when the market gets nervous or excited, options get more expensive. Big news events can spike option prices even if the stock hasn't moved yet.

What Is a "Starter Position"?
Seen in today's DIS trade

A starter position means Ben bought a small portion of what he eventually wants to own. It's a way to get in without risking everything if the trade goes wrong immediately.

  • If it starts working → he adds more shares (called "adding to a winner")
  • If it fails quickly → the loss is small because he only had a starter size in

Today's DIS trade was a starter that stopped out at a loss. That's normal — even Ben takes losses. What matters is they're small losses.

The Stop Loss — Your Safety Net
The most important rule in options trading

A stop loss is a pre-decided exit price if the trade goes against you. You set it before emotion gets involved.

  • Ben bought NVDA calls at $2.45, stop at $2.00 → max loss = ~18%
  • Ben bought TSLA calls at $3.00, stop at $2.50 → max loss = ~17%
  • BMNR stop was "-30%" from entry price

Notice a pattern? Ben usually risks 15–30% of the option price on any trade. He keeps losses small so one bad trade doesn't hurt the overall account.

The hardest part
When the stop hits, you sell. No hoping, no waiting "just a little longer." The TSLA trade today stopped out fast — Ben sent the alert calmly: "That drop escalated FAST." He didn't fight it. That discipline is what protects the account.
Time Decay — The Hidden Clock
Why you can't hold options forever

Every option has an expiration date. As that date gets closer, the option loses value automatically — even if the stock doesn't move. This is called time decay.

Ben's NVDA trade expires this Friday (May 13). That's a 2-day option. He expected a fast move — and got it. If NVDA hadn't moved, that option would have lost value every hour.

  • Short expiration (days away) = cheap, high leverage, must be right quickly
  • Longer expiration (weeks/months) = more expensive, more time for the trade to work
Trade Management — Once You're In
How to handle a 5-day options trade day by day
The Golden Rule
Once your bracket order is set — entry, target, stop — your job is largely DONE. The bracket manages it for you. You don't need to watch every tick. Set it and step away.

Day by Day — 5 Day Trade

DAY 1 — ENTRY DAY
Bracket is set ✅ Check once mid-day and once at close. Is the stock still above VWAP? Still trending right direction? If yes — leave it alone. Resist the urge to tinker.
DAYS 2–3 — THE MIDDLE
This is where emotion kicks in — resist it. Ask: is the thesis still intact? Stock still above key levels? Smart money still bullish? If Ben sends a correction alert — act on it immediately. If stop hasn't triggered — trust the bracket.
DAY 4 — URGENCY STARTS
Time decay accelerates — your option loses value faster every hour now. If profitable but target hasn't hit → consider selling half and locking in profit. Move your stop up to at least your entry price — make the rest of the trade risk free.
DAY 5 — EXPIRATION DAY ⚠️
Do NOT hold to expiration hoping for a miracle. Exit by noon CT at the latest — regardless of price. Options lose value extremely fast in the final hours. A small profit beats watching it expire worthless.

The 2 Emotions That Kill Trades

😤 Greed
Target hits but you don't sell because you want more. Then it reverses and you get nothing. The bracket exists to prevent this — let it do its job.
😰 Fear
Option dips but is still above your stop. You sell early and miss the move. If stop hasn't triggered — the trade is still valid. Trust the plan you made before emotion got involved.
Ben's words on this
"Trades are employees. You are the boss. Give them a job with expectations. If they don't perform — FIRE THEM." That means: set the bracket, give the trade its parameters, and let it work. If stop hits — fire it and move on. No emotion.

Order Management in IBKR
How to modify stops and targets without canceling — faster and safer
Never Cancel and Re-Enter
Always use Modify — it keeps your bracket's OCA group intact. If you cancel and re-enter manually you could accidentally break the link between your profit target and stop loss.
METHOD 1 — Click & Drag on Chart (TWS Desktop) ⭐ Easiest
Just like TradeStation! Find your stop order line directly on the chart. Click and hold it. Drag up or down to your new price. Release and confirm. Most intuitive method — exactly what you remember.
METHOD 2 — Right Click to Modify (TWS Desktop)
In your open orders panel → Right click the stop order → Select "Modify Order" → Change the stop price → Click Transmit. Much faster than cancel and re-enter.
METHOD 3 — Modify on IBKR Mobile
Go to Portfolio tab → Find your open position → Tap the order → Tap "Modify" → Change the price → Confirm. Clean and simple on the app.
METHOD 4 — Swipe to Edit (IBKR Mobile) ⚡ Fastest on Phone
Go to Pending Orders → Swipe LEFT on the order → Tap Edit → Change price → Submit. Fastest method when you're on your phone during Golden Hour.

When to Move Your Stop

✅ Target 1 hits → Move stop UP to your entry price → Trade is now risk free
✅ Target 2 hits → Move stop UP to Target 1 price → Locking in profit
✅ Day 4 arrives → Move stop up regardless — time decay accelerating
✅ Ben sends updated stop → Modify immediately — don't wait
❌ Never move stop DOWN → "Giving it more room" is how small losses become big losses
The Only Direction Stops Move
Stops only ever move in ONE direction — UP on calls, DOWN on puts. You tighten your protection as the trade goes in your favor. You NEVER loosen it to avoid being stopped out. That's the discipline Ben teaches.

Why a Bracket Order?
A bracket order places all three legs — buy, profit target, and stop loss — simultaneously. The moment your buy fills, your protection is already armed. Whichever exit triggers first (profit target OR stop) automatically cancels the other. This is called OCA — One Cancels All. No gap, no scrambling, no emotion.
Why LIMIT not MARKET for the entry?
A Market order fills at whatever price is available — you could pay way more than expected. A Limit order only fills at your price or better. Always use Limit for options.

Strategy 1 — VWAP Pullback
Day trade the "Golden Hour" — last 2 hours of the trading day

The big idea: Find a stock that's been strong all day, wait for it to pull back to a key support line (VWAP), and buy when it bounces. You've watched it move all day — that's your data advantage.

What is VWAP?

Volume Weighted Average Price — the price where the most buying happened that day. Think of it as the market's "fair value" for that day. Stocks tend to bounce off it like a floor.

Above VWAP = bulls in control → look for call setups
Below VWAP = bears in control → be cautious with calls

The Setup — Step by Step

  • Use a 5-minute chart with the VWAP indicator turned on (purple line in Ben's charts)
  • Find a stock that was strong in the morning and held above VWAP all day
  • Wait for price to pull back down to VWAP in the afternoon
  • Buy the call option when price touches and bounces off VWAP
  • Set stop just below VWAP — if it breaks through, you're wrong
  • Target: previous high of the day as first profit target
  • Option to use: this week or next week expiration, next strike up from current price
Why the Golden Hour (2–4 PM)?
You've watched the stock move all day before making a decision. That's data. Morning trades are guesses — afternoon trades are informed. Ben strongly prefers afternoon setups for this reason.

Strategy 2 — SPY Rubber Band
Trade the market snapping back to its average after extreme moves

The big idea: The market always comes back to its average (mean). When SPY stretches too far in one direction — like a rubber band pulled tight — it almost always snaps back. You trade that snap.

The tools on your chart
■ Blue line = 21 EMA — the mean (home base, where price wants to return)
■ Green line = 1 ATR — normal daily range
■ Yellow line = 2 ATR — extended, watch for exhaustion
■ Red line = 3 ATR — extreme, 95%+ chance of reversal

The Setup

  • Watch SPY on a daily chart with the Keltner Channel indicator (set at 1, 2, and 3)
  • Wait for SPY to hit the 3 ATR extreme level (red line) — this is rare and powerful
  • If SPY is at 3 ATR below the mean → buy CALLS (expecting bounce up)
  • If SPY is at 3 ATR above the mean → buy PUTS (expecting drop back down)
  • Target: price returns to the blue mean line (21 EMA)
  • Strike: just below the mean, 2 weeks out expiration
  • Stop: only risk 25% of the option premium
ATR = Average True Range
How much a stock typically moves in a day. Think of stocks like sprinters — they run hard, then they REST. When SPY has sprinted 3x its normal daily range, it's exhausted and ready to rest (reverse). In the past year, SPY hit ±3 ATR only 6 times — and EVERY time it bounced back to the mean.
